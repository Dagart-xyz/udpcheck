Privacy policy

Revision of 10 October 2026. Site dagart.xyz, source code: github.com/Dagart-xyz/udpcheck (AGPL-3.0).

1. Who we are

The service operator is the owner of the UDPcheck project (“we”). Contact for any questions about data: draldrean@protonmail.com. The service is non-commercial and has no advertising or analytics.

2. What the service does and why it needs data

The service checks whether UDP traffic passes between your device and our servers in your country and abroad, and shows which provider blocks traffic and in which direction. For the check to work, the servers must know which address you contact them from: otherwise they have nothing to reply to and cannot build a route to you. We collect nothing beyond what is needed for that.

Database backups are encrypted and kept for up to 14 days; they hold the same set as the database itself: visitors' IPs are not there, volunteer servers' IPs are.

There are two exceptions to “IP is not written to logs”. First: the servers' system log records the addresses from which someone tries to guess a login over SSH (this is administrator login, not the site). Second: if a request to the site clearly looks like an attack (probing for service files such as .env, typical WordPress and phpMyAdmin addresses, SQL injection), the sender's address and the request itself are written to the log. Ordinary visitors never request such addresses. Whoever does it three times within ten minutes is blocked for a day or longer. These records are kept for up to 30 days and are used only to block bots and scanners automatically. Addresses of other site visitors and of checks do not get there; rate counters live in memory and disappear on restart.

4. What we do not do

  • we use no cookies, counters, advertising, third-party scripts or fonts;
  • we do not sell or pass data to third parties;
  • we do not try to find out who you are;
  • we do not send your IP to third-party geolocation services: the geolocation databases are kept by us; network names are requested from RIPEstat and PeeringDB by network number only.

5. Who sees your IP

  • our hub (the site's server) and the project's anchor servers, which need to reply to you and build a route. They are in different countries, including outside yours, because foreign UDP cannot be checked otherwise;
  • up to two checked volunteer servers per browser check: your browser sends them short UDP packets (STUN), so their owners can technically see the address;
  • your provider and intermediate networks see the traffic itself just as they see any other traffic.

6. Basis and your consent

By pressing “Check my network” you ask the service to run a check and agree to data processing to the extent described; it is also our legitimate interest in operating the service. If you do not agree, do not press the button: the statistics pages can be read, your IP is also visible to the server for the duration of the request, but it is stored nowhere. You can withdraw consent at any time: clear the site data in your browser and do not run the check.

7. Your rights

You have the right to learn what data about you exists, to demand its correction or deletion, and to object to processing. Because we do not identify you, records of a specific person cannot be found by name or address, and so they cannot be provided or deleted. If you want your browser's records removed, send us the value of udpc_cid from your browser storage (on the page: F12 → Application / Storage → Local Storage → udpc_cid) and we will delete the related records. If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you can also contact your data protection supervisory authority.

8. Security

Connections use HTTPS, servers are accessible by keys only, backups are encrypted, and the source code is open, so how the service works can be checked (privacy principles). The service is not intended for children.

9. Changes

When the policy changes we publish a new revision with the date at the top of the page. Earlier revisions are kept in the repository history.
